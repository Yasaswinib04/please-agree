**One-liner:**
LIT School is a challenge-based learning institution that trains students in marketing, content creation, and entrepreneurship through 30–70 real brand challenges, replacing traditional exams with portfolio-building projects judged by industry practitioners.

**How it works:**
Students progress through a three-arena methodology: (1) **Learn Arena** — lecturers break down real industry challenges and professional problem-solving methods; (2) **Innovate Arena** — teams build solutions (strategies, campaigns, systems) with mentor guidance; (3) **Transform Arena** — students present to real judges and receive feedback, with every project becoming portfolio proof. Beyond curriculum, students complete real-world gigs, attend event-triggered "Pulse" classes, develop life skills (e.g., fixing tires, basic finances), follow fitness routines, and analyze live case studies. Mentors are active industry practitioners who provide briefs, review work, and connect students to opportunities.

**Who it's for:**
Students after 12th grade or during college who want practical, job-ready skills in marketing, content, or entrepreneurship instead of (or alongside) traditional degree programs. Ideal for learners who value shipped work and portfolio proof over grades and certificates.

**Differentiator:**
LIT replaces memorization and case-study theory with live execution on real brand briefs. Students build actual campaigns under mentor guidance from practitioners at Google, Zerodha, Shopify, and 500+ startup/creator partnerships. The outcome is a portfolio of shipped work, not a transcript — positioning graduates as "ready to ship, start, or freelance" from day one.

## What to pitch — and what NOT to

**DO pitch:**
- "Students build 30–70 real brand campaigns, not case studies" — concrete, differentiating
- "Mentors are practitioners from Google, Zerodha, Shopify who give live briefs and connect students to opportunities" — credible and specific
- "Portfolio-first model: every project judged by industry, no grades" — clear alternative to traditional education
- "Six-dimension growth: curriculum + gigs + live event classes + life skills + fitness + case studies" — holistic and unique
- Partnership opportunities: "Your students get real client work; our students get your brand as a portfolio piece"

**DON'T pitch:**
- The 93% placement stat without independent verification — it's a marketing claim, not measured proof
- "India's first challenge-based learning school" — superlative claims are hard to verify and sound like hype
- Comparisons that trash traditional degrees without acknowledging their value (e.g., accreditation, broad knowledge base)
- Over-promising employability without showing longitudinal data on graduate outcomes

## Objections to expect

1. **"Is LIT accredited? Will employers recognize it?"**
   - *Answer:* LIT is not a traditional degree program; it's a skills accelerator. Graduates compete on portfolio proof (live campaigns, client work) rather than certificates. Many employers in startups, agencies, and creator economy prioritize demonstrated ability over accreditation. We can connect you with hiring partners who've hired LIT alumni.

2. **"How do we know students actually get jobs?"**
   - *Answer:* The website claims 93% placement, but we're transparent: that's self-reported and we're building independent tracking. What we *can* show is the portfolio work students ship, mentor testimonials, and the 500+ partner network. Let's pilot with a cohort and measure outcomes together.

3. **"Our students need a degree for visa/further study."**
   - *Answer:* LIT is complementary, not a replacement. Students can do LIT alongside or after a degree to build job-ready skills. Think of it as the practical layer that makes the degree valuable in the market.

4. **"This sounds expensive."**
   - *Answer:* We're outcome-focused. If your students land roles or freelance gigs faster, the ROI is clear. Let's discuss partnership models — maybe your institution co-hosts challenges or shares placement fees.

5. **"What if students can't handle real client pressure?"**
   - *Answer:* That's the point. The three-arena model scaffolds them: Learn (method), Innovate (build with mentors), Transform (present and iterate). Failure is part of the process, and mentors guide them through it. By the end, they've survived real feedback — that's the skill employers pay for.

6. **"How is this different from internships?"**
   - *Answer:* Internships are one-off and often observational. LIT gives students 30–70 challenges across marketing, content, and product, with structured mentorship and a portfolio that proves range. It's internships at scale, with a learning methodology.
